# Purpose

This document defines the principles that guide my development as a software engineer.

Programming languages will change.

Frameworks will change.

AI models will change.

My engineering principles should remain stable.

This constitution is not a set of rules imposed from outside.

It is a commitment I make to myself.

Whenever I feel lost, frustrated or uncertain, I should return to this document.

---

# My Goal

I do not want to become someone who merely writes code.

I want to become an engineer who understands problems, designs solutions and builds software that is reliable, maintainable and useful.

Code is one of my tools.

Thinking is my primary skill.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. Understanding Comes Before Memorization

I do not aim to memorize syntax.

I aim to understand ideas.

If I understand the concept, syntax can always be looked up.

---

## 2. Thinking Comes Before Coding

I will not immediately begin writing code.

Before touching the keyboard, I will ask:

- What problem am I solving?
- What information do I have?
- What result do I expect?
- What edge cases should I consider?

Only after I understand the problem will I begin implementing it.

---

## 3. Curiosity Is More Valuable Than Speed

Learning quickly is not my goal.

Learning deeply is.

If I do not understand something, I will slow down instead of pretending I understand.

Questions are progress.

Not weakness.

---

## 4. Mistakes Are Data

An error message is not a failure.

It is information.

Every bug teaches me something about how the computer actually works.

Instead of asking:

"Why doesn't this work?"

I will ask:

"What assumption of mine turned out to be incorrect?"

---

## 5. Readability Is a Feature

Code is read far more often than it is written.

Future Me is also another programmer.

I write code that Future Me can understand.

If I need to choose between cleverness and clarity,

I choose clarity.

---

## 6. Simplicity Wins

I prefer the simplest solution that correctly solves the problem.

Complexity must always justify its existence.

---

## 7. Refactoring Is Improvement

Rewriting code is not admitting failure.

It is part of engineering.

Every version of my code teaches me something that the previous version could not.

---

## 8. One Responsibility at a Time

Every function,

every module,

every class,

should have one clear purpose.

If something is difficult to explain,

it is probably trying to do too much.

---

## 9. I Learn by Building

Theory matters.

Practice matters more.

Whenever possible, I connect new knowledge to real projects.

Every project becomes part of my education.

---

## 10. AI Is My Mentor, Not My Brain

AI is one of the most powerful tools ever created.

I use it to:

- learn;
- explore;
- review;
- discuss;
- accelerate development.

I do not outsource my thinking.

The final responsibility for every technical decision remains mine.

---

## 11. I Accept Not Knowing

No engineer knows everything.

Professional engineers know how to learn.

Whenever I encounter an unfamiliar topic,

I treat it as the next opportunity to grow.

---

## 12. Every Module Is a Step Forward

Progress is not measured by perfection.

It is measured by understanding today something that I did not understand yesterday.

Small improvements accumulate.

---

# My Responsibilities

As a student I commit to:

- asking questions;
- writing code myself whenever possible;
- reading my own code;
- reviewing mistakes honestly;
- keeping my Knowledge Base updated;
- treating programming as a craft rather than a school subject.

---

# Definition of Success

Success is not finishing the course.

Success is reaching the point where I can independently:

- understand a problem;
- design a solution;
- evaluate alternatives;
- implement the solution;
- verify that it works;
- improve it over time.

When I reach that point,

the language I use will no longer matter.

---

# Long-Term Vision

My goal is not simply to become a Python developer.

I want to become an engineer capable of learning any language, any framework and any technology.

Programming is only the first discipline.

Engineering thinking is the real destination.

---

# Personal Motto

> Understanding creates independence.

Everything else follows.
